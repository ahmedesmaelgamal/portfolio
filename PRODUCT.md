## Users

Primary audience: hiring managers, tech leads, and startup founders evaluating a backend engineer. Secondary: freelance clients looking for a reliable developer. They want to quickly assess technical competence, project range, and professionalism.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that functions as a living CV. Showcases projects, skills, and experience. Must feel like it was built by someone who takes craft seriously. Not a generic template. Every section should communicate competence and attention to detail.

## Register

brand

## Brand Personality

Technical. Precise. Understated confidence. Not flashy, not corporate. The kind of engineer who writes clean code and doesn't need to shout about it. Warm but not casual. Professional but not stiff.

## Anti-References

- No generic "creative agency" aesthetics (big gradients, blob shapes, floating orbs)
- No glassmorphism everywhere
- No monospace-as-lazy-shorthand for "developer"
- No hero-metric template (big number + small label + gradient accent)
- No identical card grids with icon + heading + text
- No editorial-magazine aesthetic (display serif italic + drop caps + broadsheet grid)
- No template-y "About Me" with stock photo and generic bio
- No neon-on-black "hacker" aesthetic
- No over-animated everything — motion should be purposeful, not decorative

## Strategic Principles

1. **Show, don't tell.** The code structure, component design, and attention to detail in the portfolio itself IS the proof of competence.
2. **Fast scan, deep dive.** Hiring managers spend 30 seconds. Make the first screen count. Let curious visitors go deeper.
3. **Project depth over breadth.** 5 real projects with real detail beat 20 shallow cards.
4. **Dark mode default.** This is a developer portfolio. The ambient scene: a tech lead reviewing candidates on a 27-inch monitor at night. Dark surfaces with careful color tinting.
5. **Typography as voice.** One well-chosen family with committed weight/size contrast. No reflex fonts.
